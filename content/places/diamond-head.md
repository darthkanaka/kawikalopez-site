Lēʻahi, better known as Diamond Head, is the crater at the east end of Waikīkī and the most recognizable landmark in Honolulu. The English name comes from sailors who mistook the calcite crystals on its slopes for diamonds.

[Nani Waikīkī](print:naniwaikiki) is Kawika's most popular print. It was made on a whim late one afternoon, when the sky turned pink over Waikīkī, and stitched together from nearly 25 frames. [Kaimana](print:kaimana), which means diamond, shows the crater in gold at sunrise, and [Lēʻahi Kahiko](print:leahikahiko) looks down into it from the air.

[Mauka i Makai](print:043), from the mountains to the sea, takes the long view: from the ridges above Pālolo Valley, across the whole city to Diamond Head and the ocean beyond.
