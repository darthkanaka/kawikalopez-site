Kaiwi coast and Hanauma Bay prints

Prints of Oʻahu's Kaiwi coast, from Hanauma Bay at sunrise to the tide pools, the Sandy Beach shorebreak and the highway winding along the cliffs.

The Kaiwi coast is the dry, rocky stretch of southeast Oʻahu between Hanauma Bay and Makapuʻu, with Kalanianaʻole Highway winding along the cliffs above the water. It's often called one of the most scenic shorelines in the state, and it's a short drive from town.

Hanauma looks down into the bay at sunrise, stitched together from nearly 20 frames. Kaiwi follows the highway's curves from straight above. Down at the waterline, Frosted Tidepool turns the ripples in a tide pool to frosted glass with a slow shutter, and ʻEleu catches a wave breaking at Sandy Beach as the sun comes up. Kākau is the eroded face of Koko Head from the air, its lines like a traditional Hawaiian tattoo.

At the eastern end of the coast is Makapuʻu.
